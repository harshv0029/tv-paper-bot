CHAPTER 5 - "TRADING WITH THE TREND" (fully read, fully testable)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

STAGE ANALYSIS (credited in the book to Stan Weinstein's own earlier work,
Minervini's application of it): every stock's price history cycles through
four stages -
  Stage 1 - Neglect/consolidation: sideways, oscillating around the
    200-day (40-week) MA, no real trend. Avoid buying here.
  Stage 2 - Advancing/accumulation: the ONLY stage Minervini buys in.
    Institutional volume support, higher-highs/higher-lows staircase.
  Stage 3 - Topping/distribution: increased volatility, largest one-day/
    one-week decline since the stage 2 advance began, MA rolling over.
  Stage 4 - Declining/capitulation: price mostly below a downtrending
    200-day MA, lower-highs/lower-lows, avoid/short.

TREND TEMPLATE - the 8-criterion long-only entry qualifier, ALL 8 required
together (this is the single most directly testable rule in the book):
  1. Close > 150-day SMA AND close > 200-day SMA
  2. 150-day SMA > 200-day SMA
  3. 200-day SMA has been rising for >= 1 month (ideally 4-5 months)
  4. 50-day SMA > 150-day SMA AND 50-day SMA > 200-day SMA
  5. Close > 50-day SMA
  6. Close >= 1.30x the trailing 52-week low (many of the best setups are
     2x-4x+ above their 52-week low before the move that matters even
     starts)
  7. Close <= 1.0x and >= 0.75x the trailing 52-week high (within 25% of a
     new high - closer is better)
  8. Relative strength vs the peer universe (book uses IBD's proprietary
     RS Rank, 0-100 scale) >= 70, ideally 80s-90s

BASE COUNTING: within an ongoing stage-2 uptrend, price advances in
"waves" separated by sideways bases (5-26 weeks each, typically). Base #1
and #2 (coming off a market correction) are the highest-quality/earliest
entries; by base #4-#5 the trend is well-known and failure risk is
materially higher. Not itself a numeric rule, more a risk-context overlay
on top of the Trend Template.

CHAPTER 10 - "A PICTURE IS WORTH A MILLION DOLLARS" / VCP (fully read
the VCP section; rest of the chapter - other chart patterns - NOT read)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

VOLATILITY CONTRACTION PATTERN (VCP) - the book's own name for its central
entry-timing concept. Once a stock already qualifies under the Trend
Template, VCP identifies the specific low-risk entry point within an
ongoing stage-2 base:
  - A base forms as a sequence of pullback "legs" (high -> low), each one
    progressively shallower than the last, left to right. Book calls each
    leg a contraction or "T" (as in "4T" = four contractions).
  - Typical progression example given in the book: ~25% first pullback ->
    ~15% -> ~8%, i.e. each leg roughly HALF (plus/minus) of the prior
    one's depth - not an exact ratio, a rule of thumb.
  - Usually 2-4 contractions, occasionally up to 5-6.
  - Volume should also contract through the base, especially drying up
    markedly on the final/tightest leg (the book's "line of least
    resistance" idea - few sellers left, so small demand moves price a
    lot).
  - The "technical footprint" shorthand the book uses to describe a base
    at a glance: {weeks-in-base}{deepest-leg}/{shallowest-leg}{count of
    legs}T, e.g. "40W 31/3 4T" = a 40-week base, deepest leg 31%,
    shallowest leg 3%, 4 total contractions.
  - ENTRY ("pivot buy point"): breakout above the high that precedes the
    FINAL (tightest) contraction, ideally on a volume increase. Stop goes
    just below that final contraction's low - a small, well-defined risk
    because the base itself has already done the work of squeezing out
    weak holders.
  - Variant bases mentioned but NOT VCP by this definition: a flat/box
    base (Darvas-style, 4-7 weeks, ~10-15% range, no real contraction).

CHAPTER 6 - "CATEGORIES, INDUSTRY GROUPS, AND CATALYSTS" (read the
opening ~1/3, company-categorization section; same-store-sales retail
metric section skimmed)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Six informal company categories the book says most stocks fall into:
market leaders, top competitors, institutional favorites, turnarounds,
cyclicals, past leaders/laggards. "Market leader" (#1-3 in sales/earnings
in a growing category, scalable growth, expanding margins) is the book's
stated favorite hunting ground.

Retail-specific diligence point: same-store-sales (comps) growth,
double-digit-or-more considered healthy, >25-30% considered unsustainable;
watch franchise-heavy store-count growth as a red flag if it outpaces
~100 new stores/year.

CHAPTER 12 - "RISK MANAGEMENT PART 1: THE NATURE OF RISK" (read
through the numeric loss-cap section; "Accepting the Market's
Judgments"/disposition-effect section onward NOT yet read)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Core numeric rule: hard stop-loss ceiling of 10% on any position, with an
average REALIZED loss targeted well below that, around 6-7% - justified
by loss-recovery asymmetry (a 10% loss needs +11% to recover; a 50% loss
needs +100%; the book's own worked "Loss Adjustment Exercise" example:
capping a real historical trade record's losses at -10% turned a -12.05%
compounded result into +79.89%, with the same gains and just the losses
capped).

Also: once a position shows a paper gain, the discipline is to tighten
the stop toward breakeven rather than giving the position the same
downside room it had at entry ("yesterday's profit is part of today's
principal," not house money). And: closing out or reassessing a position
promptly if it does not do what was expected shortly after entry, even
before it technically shows a loss.

REALITY CHECK (not a backtest, just a comparison, 2026-09-29): the live
intraday engine's default stop_pct is 1.0% (NSE_STOCK_DEFAULT_PARAMS,
main.py:6184) - far tighter than this book's 10% ceiling, but the two
aren't directly comparable: intraday 5-minute bars have much smaller
natural price swings than the multi-week swings this book is written
for. The swing-side strategies already live/tested in this codebase
(Gap and Go) and the new VCP research already use structural/ATR-based
stops, not a fixed small percentage, so they're the more relevant
comparison point if this 10%-ceiling idea is ever tested directly.

SIMPLIFICATIONS from the book's full description (flagged, not silently
glossed over):
  - VCP detection here only checks the breakout day's own volume (>1.5x
    its 20-day average), not the full base's volume-contraction
    signature leg by leg, which the book weighs heavily.
  - Contraction "tightening" is checked as (first considered leg's depth
    >= 2x the final leg's depth) and (final leg's depth <= 15%), using up
    to the last 6 legs found by a +/-5-bar fractal swing detector over a
    15-130 trading day lookback - NOT a strict monotonically-decreasing
    check across every intermediate leg (real price data is noisier than
    the book's clean illustrative examples).
  - Relative strength (Trend Template criterion #8) is a same-universe
    percentile-rank proxy, not the book's real IBD RS Rank.
  - Both variants use ONE exit mechanic (chandelier trail + max hold) for
    a fair entry-only comparison - not the book's own more nuanced sell
    rules (Chapter 13, not yet read/implemented).

CHAPTER 10 (REST) - OTHER CHART PATTERNS (2026-09-29, second pass -
fully read now, was VCP-section-only before)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

POWER PLAY / HIGH TIGHT FLAG - the single most distinct NEW strategy
candidate from this pass. A momentum/velocity setup, explicitly NOT
gated by the Trend Template or fundamentals (book's own words: "the
only situation I will enter with a dearth of fundamentals"). Three
numeric criteria, all required:
  1. Explosive prior move: price up >=100% in under 8 weeks on very
     high volume, after a period of relative dormancy.
  2. Consolidation/"flag": sideways for 3-6 weeks (some as short as
     12 days), correcting no more than 20-25% from the peak.
  3. Final tightening: the last leg corrects no more than 10%, OR the
     consolidation shows VCP-style leg-by-leg contraction.
  Entry (pivot): breakout above the high of the consolidation/flag on
  a volume increase. No 150/200-day SMA maturity requirement (young,
  fast-moving stocks explicitly qualify) - this is what makes it a
  genuinely different strategy from tt_vcp, not a variant of it.
  WORTH IMPLEMENTING AND TESTING: fully mechanical (prior-N-week
  return, consolidation depth/duration, breakout trigger) - no
  fundamentals data gap like chapters 7-9. Natural candle sizes: daily
  (book's own timeframe, weeks-scale) plus a 1h/4h variant per the
  standing candle-size thumb rule, since "100% in under 8 weeks" could
  plausibly compress to something like "50%+ in a session" at
  intraday granularity - worth checking rather than assuming daily-only.

CUP-WITH-HANDLE / "3C" (cup completion cheat) - a more specific named
sub-shape of the same VCP concept already implemented (tt_vcp), not a
separate edge: prior move +25% to +300% over 3-36 months, above a
rising 200-day MA, cup correction 15-50% (60%+ "too deep, prone to
failure"), duration 3-45 weeks (most 7-25), handle forms in the upper
third of the cup and corrects less than the cup itself. The "3C cheat"
entry (buying at the LOW of the cup, before the handle even forms, for
an early partial position) is the one genuinely distinct piece - an
EARLIER, more aggressive entry trigger than tt_vcp's current
breakout-above-final-contraction-high rule. Lower priority than Power
Play since it's mostly redundant with tt_vcp; the cheat-entry timing
variant is the only piece worth isolating for a test.

THE LIVERMORE SYSTEM (two-pullback confirmation) - Jesse Livermore's
entry timing, cited approvingly: don't buy the first breakout above a
downtrend line. Wait for the stock to (a) break the downtrend, (b)
pull back (reaction low #1), (c) rally again, (d) pull back again
(reaction low #2), then buy only once price exceeds the SECOND
reaction high. Explicitly framed as reducing whipsaw/false-breakout
losses at the cost of a later, worse entry price. WORTH TESTING as an
entry-timing FILTER layered on top of any existing breakout-based
entry (tt_vcp, gap_and_go, universal_score, idea4) - "does requiring a
second confirmed higher high improve win rate enough to offset the
worse average entry price" is a clean, isolated one-axis test in this
session's own established style.

FAILURE RESET / "PIVOT RESET" - not a new pattern, but a repeatedly
emphasized MECHANIC across chapters 10, 11, and 13 (see REENTRY note
below) - being stopped out doesn't disqualify a symbol; if it resets
and re-triggers a valid signal (sometimes within days - a "pivot
reset" - sometimes needing a whole new base - a "base reset"), the
second setup is often described as stronger than the first ("some of
my biggest winners were stocks that stopped me out and then reset").

CHAPTER 11 - "DON'T JUST BUY WHAT YOU KNOW" (2026-09-29, fully read,
was not yet read before)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

PRIMARY BASE - buy a recently-IPO'd stock as it emerges from its FIRST
buyable base, not a stage-2 continuation base. Genuinely distinct from
tt_vcp because it deliberately targets YOUNG stocks that likely don't
even have 150/200 days of trading history yet (so can't pass the
Trend Template at all) - the book's own framing: "most superperformers
go public within 8 to 10 years before the start of their
superperformance phase," and youth is a "vital ingredient."
  - Base forms during a correction of 3+ weeks (up to ~1 year for a
    deeper reset), not correcting more than 25-35% for a short (3-5
    week) base, or up to ~50% for a longer one; corrections beyond
    that are described as "unbuyable" (book's own Facebook IPO 2012
    counterexample: -43% in 12 days, never set up properly).
  - Entry: breakout to a new ALL-TIME high (since IPO) from that base,
    on a volume increase.
  WORTH TESTING: needs an "IPO recency" proxy since this codebase has
  no direct IPO-date field - candidate proxy is simply how much price
  history exists before the current bar (e.g. first available bar via
  a "max" period fetch), treating a short available history as a
  recent-listing signal. Full-universe NSE test would need to actually
  measure how many NSE_FULL_UNIVERSE symbols have short histories in
  the data source before this is worth building - flag that as a
  quick feasibility check before committing to the full build/test
  cycle.

CHAPTER 13 - "RISK MANAGEMENT PART 2" (2026-09-29, fully read, was not
yet read before)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Mostly psychology/discipline narrative (contingency planning, honoring
stops, avoiding averaging down, position-count/diversification
philosophy of 4-6 to 10-20 names) with three concretely testable
mechanics:

  1. BREAKEVEN-STOP AT 2R/3R (the precise version of chapter 12's
     fuzzy "breakeven ratchet" note above): once unrealized gain
     reaches 2x-3x the position's OWN initial risk (entry minus
     initial stop), move the stop to breakeven, full stop - not a
     trailing/chandelier mechanism. Simpler and more literal than the
     chandelier trail (running peak close - ATR mult) already used for
     tt_vcp/idea4. WORTH TESTING as an alternative exit against the
     existing chandelier trail on whichever entry (tt_vcp, gap_and_go,
     idea4) is already validated - a clean, isolated exit-only swap.

  2. SCALE-IN POSITION CONSTRUCTION (also flagged, vaguely, in the
     "MULTI-BASE PYRAMIDING" note above - chapter 13 gives the actual
     mechanism): split entry into 2-3 tranches (e.g. 2%/2%/1% of
     capital) added ONLY as price confirms with an open profit -
     "never trust the first price unless the position shows a profit"
     - stop set off the blended average cost, never averaging DOWN
     into a loss. WORTH TESTING as a sizing variant against today's
     single-shot flat/confidence-scaled entry.

  3. REENTRY / no cooldown after a stop-out (ties together the
     FAILURE RESET note above) - don't blacklist a symbol after being
     stopped out; if the same signal fires again shortly after,
     reenter. WORTH A QUICK CHECK first: does main.py's scheduler
     already implicitly allow same-day/next-scan reentry on a fresh
     signal, or is there an unintentional cooldown/blacklist
     suppressing it? Cheap to verify before treating this as new work.
